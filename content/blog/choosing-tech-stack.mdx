"What tech stack should we use?" is the question we hear most from clients starting new projects. It's also the question with the most wrong answers floating around the internet — because the right stack depends entirely on your specific context.

## The Decision Framework

We evaluate tech stack choices across five dimensions: team expertise, project requirements, scalability needs, ecosystem maturity, and total cost of ownership. No single dimension should dominate — it's the combination that matters.

## Team Expertise Trumps Trends

The best technology is the one your team knows well. A mediocre framework in the hands of an expert team will outperform a cutting-edge framework in the hands of beginners every time. If your team has deep React experience, don't switch to Svelte because it benchmarks 15% faster — the productivity loss will cost you far more than the performance gain.

## Match Architecture to Scale

A startup serving 1,000 users has fundamentally different architectural needs than a platform serving 10 million. Start with the simplest architecture that meets your current needs (often a monolith), and plan for the seams where you'll split into services later. Premature microservices architecture is one of the most expensive mistakes we see.

## The Ecosystem Factor

Frameworks don't exist in isolation. Consider the ecosystem: package availability, community size, hiring pool, IDE support, hosting options, and long-term maintenance. A technology with a large, active community will serve you better than a technically superior alternative with a small, niche following.

## Our Current Recommendations

For web applications, we typically recommend Next.js with TypeScript — it handles everything from static sites to complex applications with excellent developer experience and performance. For mobile, Flutter has emerged as the clear cross-platform leader. For backend services, Node.js or Go depending on performance requirements. For infrastructure, AWS unless you have a specific reason to choose otherwise.

## The Stack Is Not the Product

Remember: your users don't care what programming language you used. They care about speed, reliability, and experience. The best tech stack is the one that lets your team ship a great product quickly and maintain it confidently over time.
